📋 90-day action plan for launching in Europe

Why 90 days instead of 30? Because the critical path in Europe is the VAT number: in practice VAT applications take 2–4 months, and without a VAT number you cannot compliantly send inventory to FBA. The right approach is to start tax and compliance first, and run account setup, listing, and content creation in parallel — don't wait for one item to finish before starting the next.
T-90 daysStart first
  • Start VAT number applications (engage a tax agent; EORI is applied for at the same time) — apply in whichever country you decide to store inventory
  • Start your compliance review: use the "Compliance Reference" tool to check which tests your category must pass (see 05)
  • If you plan to sell in ES: the POA must be notarized — start this first (it's the most time-consuming step)
Weeks 1–2
  • Get a registration link from your account manager → complete account registration (new sellers) / open the European stores from your existing Seller Central (existing sellers)
  • Start gathering KYC documents (articles of incorporation, shareholding structure, bill dated within 90 days…) and submit them
  • Set up your deposit method and switch two-step verification to Google Authenticator
Weeks 3–5
  • Apply for a trademark / authorize your brand identity from another marketplace (you can use brand tools first without waiting for the trademark to be granted)
  • EPR registration: register one by one for each "country of sale × category," then upload the numbers to the compliance portal in Seller Central
  • Designate an EU responsible person (EU RP) and disclose it on your listings; if you sell in the UK, check your category's regulations to see whether a UK responsible person is required
  • Prepare listing content and submit it for official free translation (LOSG / BIL / GSO)
Weeks 6–9
  • Use the Revenue Calculator to estimate FBA costs and selling prices (including local VAT)
  • List your first batch of products (cross-marketplace sellers can sync with LOSG / BIL)
  • Talk to first-mile logistics providers; confirm the customs clearance method and required compliance documents
Weeks 10–12Once VAT numbers are in place
  • VAT number uploaded to Seller Central and verified → create your first FBA shipment (packing, FNSKU labels, first-mile dispatch)
  • Enroll in Vine, launch Sponsored Products ads, set up coupons
  • Confirm that each new seller benefit has been triggered (the "Incentives" page in Seller Central)
✅ You don't have to wait for every VAT number before you start selling. As long as you hold FBA inventory in one country (e.g. DE) and have that country's VAT number, you can use EFN / remote fulfillment to sell to buyers on the other EU stores, and those orders remain Prime-eligible. For B2C orders sold through Amazon to EU consumers where the goods are already inside the EU, VAT for sellers established outside the EU is generally collected and remitted by Amazon (deemed supplier rules). Whether cross-border B2C sales through other channels should go through OSS — please confirm with your tax agent (see 04). Suggested path: start with EFN from a single country to validate demand → once volume grows, add VAT numbers and move to local inventory / Pan-EU.

❓ FAQ

How is the referral fee credit calculated? Could it go unused?

Using the UK as an example, it works in two tiers: on the first £40,000 of eligible branded sales, each order earns a referral fee credit of 10% of the order value; on the next £760,000, it's 5%. The total is capped at £42,000. The EU-4 cap is €47,250; check the NSI page in Seller Central for the EUR thresholds of each tier.

Example: Order £100, referral fee rate 8% = referral fee £8. The credit at 10% of the order value = £10, but each order's credit is the lower of the referral fee and the credit amount, so £8 is actually offset and the referral fee you pay on that order is £0.

⏱️ The prepaid credit expires 12 months after it is issued. No action is needed; it is applied automatically to qualifying orders.

Timing is critical for the Vine incentive. How do I get it right?

You must enroll in Vine within 90 days of completing Brand Registry (or within 90 days of your first buyable ASIN going live, whichever is later) to get this fee credit. There are three different deadlines here:

  • Enrollment deadline: join Vine within 90 days of becoming eligible for brand benefits.
  • Credit expiry: the credit only expires if it goes unused for one year.
  • First-five-days rule: if you enroll ASINs in Vine within 5 days of becoming eligible, you still receive the credit, but the Vine enrollment fees for those ASINs are charged in full; only ASINs enrolled after day 5 use the credit.

Vine is Amazon's trusted reviewer program: you provide free products to reviewers, and they write honest reviews (which may be positive or negative). It is very effective for building early reviews on new products.

How do I get the most out of the ad credit tiers?

You need to set up an ad campaign within 90 days of your first buyable ASIN going live. Credits are then issued in three tiers based on your cumulative ad spend over 30 days (UK): spend £37–149.99, get £37; spend £150–749.99, get £150; spend £750 or more, get £750. For EU-4 tier amounts, check Seller Central.

The credit is added to your advertising account within 14 days after the 30-day accumulation period ends, and expires if unused within 30 days of being issued, so have a campaign plan ready once you receive it instead of letting it lapse.

⚠️ Eligibility timing for the new ad benefit: for both the UK and the EU-4, Sponsored Products campaigns must be created after 2025/08/20 (Pacific Time).

All sellersKYC is unique to Amazon's European stores
📌 Key takeaway: In Europe, one "European Unified Account" runs both the UK and the EU marketplaces, and the monthly subscription fee is charged only once. The real hurdle is the KYC seller verification that starts after registration. This process is unique to Amazon's European stores, and incomplete documents lead to repeated rejections that can stall your entire launch timeline.
🇪🇺 What is the European Unified Account?
A single account lets you sell on European marketplaces such as the UK, Germany, France, Italy and Spain. You can switch freely between marketplaces in Seller Central, and the Professional seller subscription fee is charged only once (see 03 Cost Estimate).
📌 The European Unified Account also covers marketplaces such as the Netherlands, Sweden and Poland. This handbook focuses on the five core marketplaces above; reach out to me if you want to add other marketplaces.

⚠️ But note: since Brexit, the UK and the EU are two separate systems for tax, compliance and inventory. UKCA and CE are different marks, UK EPR and EU EPR require separate registrations, and UK FBA inventory and EU FBA inventory are two independent inventory pools that cannot be transferred between each other (you must send inventory to FBA separately, or use Remote Fulfilment with FBA).

📌 "Primary operating address" verification (most sellers don't know about this yet)

The "primary operating address" and the "registered address" on your company registration are two different fields; they can be the same or different. Businesses should enter the physical address where the main day-to-day business activities take place (for example, where administration happens and where employees work); sole proprietors / individual sellers use their residential address as the primary operating address. Amazon uses it to determine your tax establishment status in the EU / UK, which in turn determines how VAT and other taxes are handled.
  • ✅ Taiwanese sellers should simply enter their actual place of business. You don't need to, and shouldn't, enter a European address. Sellers whose primary operating address is outside the EU / UK may be asked to provide supporting documents.
  • 📌 The review is completed within 3 days of submitting all required information; it will not start until everything has been submitted. The verification method and required documents are as stated in the Seller Central notification.
  • ⚠️ When asked to verify, the deadline is usually 60–90 days, as shown on the Account Health page; if you miss it, your disbursements will be held and your account deactivated.
  • ⚠️ The address must be a physical location where you actually operate; a mail-forwarding-only address does not meet the official definition.
Source: Understanding your primary operating address to sell in the EU and UK. For actual requirements, follow the Seller Central notification.
⚠️ Changing account information in Seller Central re-triggers KYC. Changes to address, email or phone all trigger a new review. In practice, a common outcome is that the account gets restricted from selling before the documents are complete. So: don't change these fields during peak-season stocking or right before a major sales event; if you really need to change them, prepare the documents first.
⚠️ Most rejections are not about eligibility; they're about document formatting. Check each item before submitting:
  • Everything must be a color scan (no black-and-white copies)
  • No e-bills, no screenshots
  • No signs of editing, no watermarks; scans must not be blurry or have glare
  • When scanning, the document's edges must not line up with the edges of the file; a margin must be visible on all four sides, or the document will be treated as incomplete
  • Consistent name romanization: the English spelling on your ID, the registration form and Seller Central must match exactly. In practice, many rejections come down to different spellings (for example, the passport uses CHU, Seller Central says Chu Hsiang-Chih, and the registration form uses yet another spelling).
  • Upload documents for every beneficial owner: a common rejection is "only some BOs were uploaded." The system checks against every beneficial owner listed in the company registration.
  • 📌 Document age: in practice, documents older than about 90 days are often sent back for resubmission (Amazon has not formally published this threshold, but cases are consistent).
  • Never submit falsified documents. KYC is a financial-regulation check; the consequence of falsification is not a rejection but suspension.
No shareholder holds 25%. How do I fill in the beneficial owners?

The order is: first, identify any individual holding ≥25%; if no one reaches 25%, declare the person with actual control, or a senior executive (Chairman / CEO / CFO / Director) instead.

You can submit either of two formats: a UBO Declaration Letter, or an Investment Chart that shows in table form that no one holds more than 25%.

The declaration letter must be on company letterhead, dated within the last 180 days, and signed by the General Counsel (most Taiwanese companies don't have this role, so ask your account manager first whether an outside lawyer or the legal representative can sign). You can request a template from your account manager.

My KYC keeps getting rejected. What should I do?

First, go through the "upload requirements" in the red box above item by item. In practice, about 80% of rejections are formatting issues (black-and-white, screenshots, watermarks, mismatched addresses, bills older than 90 days), not eligibility issues.

Then confirm three consistency checks: ① name on ID = legal representative's name on the registration form = name in Seller Central; ② company name and address on the bill = company registration (business license); ③ address on personal bill = residential address entered in Seller Central.

If you're still stuck, send a screenshot of the rejection message to your account manager. I can help interpret the rejection reason and follow up internally.

B. Europe-specific costs: separate "one-time" from "annual upkeep"

💡 This is the single most important idea when estimating European costs. Many people add two completely different kinds of spending together, arrive at a scary total, and then give up. In reality:
  • A good share of the one-time costs may already have been covered by your factory (for example, CE test reports and FCM declarations). Those are "to be confirmed," not "to be paid." Ask your suppliers for existing documents first; this often cuts out a large chunk.
  • The annual upkeep costs are the true fixed costs that build up year after year, and they're the group to focus on in your P&L model.

Core rule: VAT numbers follow your "inventory," not your "sales"

A Taiwanese company with no establishment in the EU can still use the Union OSS to report intra-EU cross-border B2C sales of "goods" (but not services: using the Union OSS for B2C services requires an EU establishment). However, you can't freely choose the registration country:

  • Registration country (Member State of Identification) = the country the goods are "dispatched from". If your FBA inventory is in Germany and ships from Germany, you register for OSS in Germany.
  • If you hold inventory in several countries, you can choose any one of the dispatch countries, but once chosen, you're locked in for "the current year + the following two full years" (about three years); you can't switch whenever you like.
  • To change the registration country, you must hold inventory in at least two member states; the change takes effect at the end of the current quarter, and the lock-in period restarts. In practice, this means "exit the old country + register again in the new one."
  • 📌 The Union OSS uses your existing VAT number in the registration country (the one you use for inventory in that country); no separate number is issued. The Non-Union OSS identification number used by non-EU businesses to report "services" is a different thing; don't mix the two up.

What this means for you: If you're sure you need OSS and your long-term main warehouse will be in Germany, don't register OSS in a temporary dispatch country just to "start selling first." That would lock you into that country's filing schedule and tax agent fees for three years. When you decide on your first FBA country, ask your tax agent about this at the same time.

Source: European Commission official guidance on the VAT One Stop Shop (Union scheme registration rules). For actual registration procedures and documents, follow your tax agent and the relevant country's tax authority.

🇪🇸🇮🇹 Two practical hurdles in Southern Europe (where many sellers give up)

🇪🇸 Spain: two-stage registration. Seller Central rejects a domestic-only number, and that's normal
For Spain, the domestic tax number and eligibility for intra-EU transactions (VIES registration) must be confirmed separately, and the order can't be reversed:
① The domestic tax number NIF (starts with N, domestic use only, not found in VIES) is issued first → ② apply separately for ROI registration; once approved, the same number with an ES prefix (NIF-IVA) can be verified in VIES and used for cross-border transactions. It is not a new, different number.

⚠️ The most common point where sellers give up: Seller Central rejects the domestic number when you enter it, but you need that number before you can apply for ROI registration. Sellers often see the rejection message, assume "the application failed," and abandon the whole process. That screen is normal.

Another format trap that can cost you a whole round of waiting: when entering the domestic number, don't add the country code: N1234567F ✓ / ESN1234567F ✗ (this returns VIES Rejected, which is a format error; delete it and upload again). The two rejection messages look very similar, but they're handled completely differently.

📅 Timeline and hard limits (these will shape your schedule):
  • The timeline has two stages: ① getting the domestic tax number; ② waiting for AEAT review after submitting ROI registration. How long each stage takes depends on the tax authority and service provider; check with your tax agent, and estimate the two stages separately when planning.
    📌 The law gives AEAT a deadline of 3 months, and no response by the deadline counts as a rejection (not an approval), so follow up proactively; don't assume no news means you're still in the queue.
  • 📌 There is no "only certain months of the year" restriction on applying (the official AEAT page sets no application window). ROI registration can be submitted at any time: on form Modelo 036, tick box 582 (request to join ROI), and enter the expected date of your first cross-border transaction in box 584.
  • 📌 In practice, applications for an EU VAT number are often asked to provide evidence of B2B orders or cross-border transactions (this is tax authority review practice, not a form field requirement). Check with your tax agent first on what evidence you have.
  • The POA must be notarized and is usually the most time-consuming step, so start it first.

⚠️ Without an EU VAT number: you can't send inventory to FBA in Spain, and any local inventory must be removed within 30 days.

Layer 3: Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)

EPR is an environmental policy that makes the "producer" responsible for the product's entire life cycle (including waste collection and treatment).

Understand this first: you are very likely the "producer." A producer is not the same as the manufacturer. If you sell EPR-regulated products in a country and you have no company established in that country, you are the producer in that country — the vast majority of Taiwanese sellers fall into this situation.

The three EPR steps (all required)

  1. Register: Register with the producer responsibility organization (PRO) for each country and category, obtain an EPR registration number, and upload it to the EPR compliance portal in Seller Central.
  2. Report: Report sales volumes (units/weight) to the PRO within the applicable reporting period.
  3. Pay: Pay the environmental fees to the PRO (monthly/quarterly/annually, depending on the category and PRO).
⚠️ Just giving Amazon your registration number does not mean you are compliant. Reporting and paying are your own ongoing obligations.
⚠️ EPR must be registered separately for "each selling country and each category"; registrations are not shared. If the same product is sold in Germany and France, you must register in both countries separately.
⚠️ Amazon paying on your behalf ≠ being compliant. In France, if you don't provide a valid UIN, Amazon will pay the environmental fees to the PRO on your behalf and charge that amount to you; in Germany, Amazon will directly suspend your non-compliant products in the EPR categories.
Example: A Bluetooth speaker sold in both Germany and France — how many EPR registrations?

Answer: 6. The product involves three categories — packaging, electrical and electronic equipment (EEE), and batteries — and Germany and France must be registered separately, so 3 × 2 = 6 registration numbers.

Another example: a garment sold in both Germany and France → 3 in total. 1 in Germany (packaging), 2 in France (packaging + textiles).
The difference: France lists textiles as an EPR category; Germany does not.

⚠️ Even if your product itself doesn't fall into any special EPR category, as long as you wrap the product in packaging or protect it during shipping, you must register for the packaging category. In practice, almost no seller is exempt.

🔋 Dangerous goods (hazmat) review: products with batteries get blocked "before you create a shipment"

This is the step sellers of battery-containing products most often miss in their timeline: it is not in the compliance chapter or the reporting obligations; it blocks you when you try to create an FBA shipment. If a product is classified as dangerous goods (Dangerous Goods / Hazmat), it must pass the dangerous goods review before it can be sent to FBA.

Which products are classified as dangerous goods (broader than most people think):
  • Anything containing lithium batteries (electronics with built-in batteries, Bluetooth devices, power tools, toys with batteries)
  • Aerosols and pressurized containers, flammable liquids (cleaners containing alcohol, perfume), flammable solids
  • Strongly magnetic products, corrosive or irritant chemicals, some skincare and beauty products
  • You must submit one of these two documents: a Safety Data Sheet (SDS / MSDS), or an Exemption Sheet, used when the product contains no hazardous components. The document must match the ASIN / SKU, and it must show the battery's chemistry, watt-hours (Wh), quantity, and packaging method.
  • First-mile shipping (especially air freight) has its own requirements: lithium batteries need a UN38.3 test report and MSDS, and must be classified under the IATA lithium battery packing instructions (PI 965–970: batteries alone / packed with equipment / contained in equipment). Lithium-ion batteries shipped on their own (UN3480 / PI 965) cannot go on passenger aircraft, only cargo aircraft, and must be kept at 30% charge or below. This directly changes your freight cost and the routes available to you.
  • Practical order: ① Ask your supplier for UN38.3 + MSDS first (most factories already have them; this is "to be confirmed," not "to be paid for") → ② Submit the dangerous goods review in Seller Central → ③ Only after approval, create the shipment and book first-mile shipping. If you change battery capacity, change packaging, or switch factories, you must resubmit for review.
  • ⚠️ What happens if you don't submit or fail the review: you cannot create a shipment; inventory already at FBA may be quarantined (blocked from sale) or need to be removed, which is very expensive to handle at an overseas warehouse. Finding out just before peak season means your whole stock replenishment is delayed.

For the scope of dangerous goods classification, required documents, and review times, check the dangerous goods identification / review pages in Seller Central and each marketplace's FBA dangerous goods policy as the official source. For air freight rules, check the current year's IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations and confirm with your freight forwarder.

① COO (country of origin) information: missing it cuts off cross-border orders (check Seller Central for the actual requirements)
This is a mandatory field you must fill in to sell, not a document kept on file. If products are shipped cross-border to buyers in the EU / UK through FBA last-mile logistics or your own fulfillment, you must provide the country of origin in Seller Central. Existing products without it will be restricted from cross-border sales ("outside the EU → into the EU" and "outside the UK → into the UK" are both blocked), and for new products, if you can't select a COO you cannot create the listing.

🔍 First check whether this applies to you: it applies if you are enrolled in any one of "Amazon global programme," "FBA Export," or "Remote Fulfilment with FBA". The paths are [Settings > Fulfillment by Amazon] (for the first two) and [Inventory > Remote Fulfilment with FBA > Marketplace Enrolment].

⚠️ The six most common mistakes:
  • It's at the SKU level, not the ASIN level. This is the most common mix-up.
  • Parent SKUs don't need it, but every child SKU does.
  • The Category Listings Report is per marketplace: if you sell on the 5 core European marketplaces (UK, DE, FR, IT, ES), you have to download, fill in, and upload it 5 times. One file does not cover all of them.
  • You cannot enter "European Union"; you must enter a specific member state (Germany, France, etc.).
  • Each SKU can have only one country of origin; if there really are multiple origins, split the SKU.
  • Used products also need it; inactive SKUs that are temporarily out of stock also need it if you plan to sell them again.
  • ✅ You don't need to upload a certificate of origin: Amazon only wants the dropdown value, not a document (but check separately whether customs requires a certificate).
  • 💡 How to decide with multiple origins? Use the country where the last "substantial transformation" took place. If unsure, ask your supplier first, then your customs broker.
  • 💡 For bulk uploads, set listing action to Edit (Partial Update). The official method is to keep only the first 5 columns (Status, Title, SKU, Product Type, Listing Action) and the COO column, and clear the data in all other cells so you don't accidentally change quantity and price.   • 📌 After everything is filled in, the compliance alert on the Seller Central home page disappears automatically within about 10 days (it doesn't clear instantly, so don't assume it hasn't taken effect).

② 🇪🇺 EU import details (MRN / EORI): required when shipping to France, or to any EU warehouse if you've enabled Pan-EU

Amazon must keep import records for non-EU sellers, so eligible sellers must provide the MRN (customs declaration number) and EORI for each shipment in Seller Central. The current rule is called "Import details collection for FBA inbounds to EU."

It applies if you meet both conditions: (1) The seller is not established in the EU (Taiwanese sellers fall into this group)　(2) You ship from outside the EU to a fulfillment center in France, or you have enabled Pan-EU and ship to a fulfillment center in any EU country.
What matters is where the goods ship from, not where the product was made. Sellers with Pan-EU enabled must provide details for shipments received at every EU fulfillment center; sellers without Pan-EU only for shipments received at fulfillment centers in France.

📅 Deadline and consequences: you must provide the MRN and EORI within 60 days of the shipment's first arrival at the warehouse. If any shipment is still missing details after the deadline, you cannot create new EU shipments; inventory already in the EU keeps selling and inbound shipments are still received. The restriction is lifted once you fill in the details.

How to submit: Seller Central → "Manage shipments" page. Each applicable shipment shows its import details status (Complete / Incomplete). Click the status to open the side panel, enter the MRN and EORI, and save.

  • EORI: you can enter your own, or the EORI of the customs broker who files the declaration for you.
  • If you don't have an MRN, choose the applicable option under "Do you have import records?":
    · "No – I purchased the goods domestically in the EU": the goods were bought within the EU (keep the formal purchase invoice; a pro forma invoice won't do)
    · "No – My broker did not provide MRN/EORI upon request": the customs broker didn't provide it. This is a temporary measure; Amazon reviews its use regularly, and you still need to get the MRN from your broker for future shipments.
  • If you use Amazon's customs clearance service, the MRN may be collected automatically through the carrier's system, but you are still responsible for import compliance.

What an MRN is: an 18-character alphanumeric customs declaration number (2-digit declaration year + 2-letter declaring country code + 14 characters, e.g. 24FR12345678654345), generated by EU customs at declaration. It is the declaration number from the goods' first country of import. If one shipment has several customs declarations, enter each MRN on a new line. Official help page

💡 Sellers can't generate an MRN themselves; you must request it from your freight forwarder / customs broker. When you create the shipment, tell your forwarder up front, "I need the MRN after clearance." Don't wait for the system reminder to go looking for it. Chasing it after the fact often drags on for a long time.
③ 🇬🇧 UK IEN (Import Entry Number): miss the deadline and you can't create new shipments

The UK FHDDS (Fulfilment House Due Diligence Scheme) requires sellers not established in the UK who send goods imported from outside the UK to a UK FC to submit an IEN (Import Entry Number) in Seller Central within 60 days of the shipment's first arrival at the warehouse.

If a valid IEN is still missing after the deadline and you haven't submitted an applicable exemption reason: you cannot create new UK FBA inbound shipments (existing inventory still sells normally). If this happens during peak-season replenishment, it directly disrupts your restocking schedule.
  • 📌 Only two situations let you skip the IEN and choose a reason option instead (on the shipment's Import details page): ① "No–I purchased the goods domestically in the UK": the goods were bought within the UK; keep the formal invoice (a pro forma invoice won't do); ② "No–My goods were imported under Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) Incoterm": imported on DDP terms; also keep documents proving DDP. Amazon shares these choices with customs for verification and may ask you to show records at any time. In all other cases you must provide the IEN.
  • The IEN is generated by UK customs at declaration and issued to the declarant; you need to request it from your freight forwarder or customs broker. If they won't provide it, you can request your customs declaration data from HMRC.
  • 💡 If you use Amazon's customs clearance service to ship to UK warehouses, the IEN is collected automatically and you don't need to enter it.
  • 💡 This is separate from the EU MRN system: shipping to the UK requires an IEN, and shipping to the EU (France, or any EU warehouse with Pan-EU) requires MRN / EORI. Each must be submitted on its own. Official help page
  • 📌 Amazon keeps records for 6 years as required, and you can look up shipments from two years ago.

🔄 Returns in Europe: 14-day no-reason returns are a legal right, not a platform policy

This is the one US/JP sellers most often get wrong. You can adjust the details of your return policy on the platform, but the right of withdrawal comes from the EU Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU) and cannot be excluded by policy.

RuleDetails (with article references)
14-day no-reason withdrawalBuyers in a distance contract have 14 days to withdraw without giving any reason; no explanation is required. The period starts from the day the buyer receives the goods (Art. 9).
No notice means 12 months⚠️ If you did not inform the buyer of the right of withdrawal in advance, the withdrawal period is extended to 12 months after the end of the initial period (Art. 10). This is the single most expensive oversight.
Refund deadline and scopeAfter the buyer gives notice of withdrawal, you must refund all payments within 14 days, including the original standard delivery cost (the extra cost of an upgraded delivery option chosen by the buyer does not need to be refunded). You may wait until you receive the returned goods, or until the buyer provides proof of sending them back, before refunding (Art. 13).
Who pays return shippingThe buyer must send the goods back within 14 days of giving notice. The buyer bears the direct cost of return shipping, but only if you informed them in advance; if you didn't, you absorb it (Art. 14).
Depreciation from opening and useThe buyer is only liable for diminished value caused by handling "beyond what is necessary to establish the nature, characteristics and functioning of the goods". If you did not inform them of the right of withdrawal, you can't claim even this from the buyer (Art. 14(2)).
ExceptionsCustom-made / personalized goods, perishable goods, sealed goods unsealed after delivery that are not suitable for return for health or hygiene reasons, sealed audio/video recordings or software that have been unsealed, goods inseparably mixed with other items, and so on (Art. 16). Sellers of personal hygiene products should check carefully whether their products fall under this exception.
💰 Three financial implications (factor these in when working out pricing and promotion ROI):
  • Returned items can't always be resold: once opened they may become used stock or be scrapped, especially apparel and electronics.
  • You must refund the original outbound shipping cost to the buyer, so each return costs you at least "outbound shipping + handling cost + possible product depreciation".
  • FBA sellers don't have to handle the process themselves, but the cost is still charged to you; FBM sellers also need to add the cost of setting up a returns route (see 09).

Source: Directive 2011/83/EU (Consumer Rights Directive) Art. 9, 10, 13, 14, 16. Details may vary slightly in how each member state has transposed it, and the platform policy may offer terms more generous than the legal minimum (for example, a longer return window). Please confirm with the marketplace return policy and your legal advisor.

FAQ ❓

All sellers
Do I need to set up a company in Europe?

No. You can register for Amazon's European marketplaces with your Taiwan company. But note: precisely because you don't have a local company, you are considered the "producer" for EPR purposes, and GPSR requires you to appoint an EU Responsible Person. These two obligations are a direct consequence of "not having a local company"; they are not optional extras.

How many European marketplaces can one account sell on? Does the monthly fee go up?

With the European Unified Account, one set of credentials lets you operate the UK and every European marketplace (this handbook focuses on the five core marketplaces: UK / DE / FR / IT / ES), and the Professional seller monthly fee is charged only once; it doesn't increase with the number of marketplaces.
But your tax and compliance obligations don't merge just because the account does: you need a VAT number in every country where you store inventory, and an EPR registration in every country you sell to.

Do I need a VAT number before I can start selling in Europe?

You only need a country's VAT number if you store inventory there. So the fastest route to start selling is: register for VAT in one country, send inventory to FBA in that one country, and use EFN to cover the other marketplaces, then decide later based on volume whether to register in more.

Don't fall into the trap of "waiting until all VAT numbers are in before selling". That can leave you waiting for months for nothing.

Amazon is paying the EPR fees on my behalf. Do I still need to register?

Yes. Paying on your behalf takes care of the "money", not the "registration obligation", and the fees paid on your behalf are ultimately charged back to you. In Germany, without a valid registration number your products in that EPR category are suspended outright; in France, Amazon pays on your behalf and charges you. For packaging under the PPWR, producers must obtain a registration number in each EU country they sell to even if they use a paid-on-behalf service; to opt out of paid-on-behalf packaging in France or Spain, you must choose self-declaration and self-payment in Seller Central. Getting a registration number does not opt you out automatically.

Also, keep two things separate: product labeling compliance (what has to appear on the product) and platform listing eligibility (EPR registration number) are different obligations. Completing one doesn't mean the other is done.

Can European buyers return items "for no reason"? Can I refuse?

Yes, they can, and you can't refuse. The EU Consumer Rights Directive gives buyers in distance contracts a 14-day no-reason right of withdrawal (counted from receipt of the goods). This is a legal right, not an Amazon policy, so it can't be excluded by a custom return policy.

⚠️ The most expensive oversight is not informing the buyer of this right in advance. In that case the withdrawal period extends from 14 days to 12 months after the end of the initial period, and the buyer isn't even liable for diminished value from opening and using the item. For details (refund deadline, who pays shipping, which products are exempt), see 12.

My product contains lithium batteries. What extra steps do I need before sending inventory to FBA?

Handle two sets of requirements separately: ① Amazon's dangerous goods (hazmat) review (submit an SDS/MSDS or an exemption sheet; if it isn't approved you can't create an FBA shipment); ② first-mile transport (lithium batteries need a UN38.3 test report and MSDS; air freight also has IATA packing classifications and route restrictions).

In practice, first ask your supplier for their existing UN38.3 and MSDS (factories usually already have them), then submit them for review in Seller Central, and only schedule first-mile shipping after approval. For steps and common mistakes, see 09; battery EPR and labeling obligations are a separate matter, see 05.

What's different about Account Health in Europe?

The biggest difference: Account Health is shared across the entire European Unified Account. A problem on one marketplace can affect your selling privileges on your other European marketplaces. This is the exact opposite of "tax and compliance are handled separately per country".

Also, violations in Europe mostly start in the product safety and compliance column (missing EU Responsible Person, missing EPR, missing test reports), not with fulfillment metrics. For each metric and its threshold, see 12.

Can the UK and EU marketplaces share inventory?

Not through automatic transfers. Since Brexit, UK FBA and EU FBA are two separate inventory pools. Either send inventory to each separately (two sets of first-mile shipping and customs clearance), or use remote fulfillment to serve one side from the other (saves on first-mile shipping but has higher fulfillment fees; per-unit price cap of €150 on EU marketplaces and £135 on the UK marketplace, and from 2026/7/1 shipments of €150 or less sent directly from outside the EU to EU consumers incur an additional €3 customs duty per item / tariff line; see 09).

Compliance is also two separate sets: UKCA vs CE, UK EPR vs EU EPR, UK Responsible Person vs EU Responsible Person.

What if I don't receive the verification text message on my phone?

International text messages are relayed through multiple carriers, and it's common for them not to arrive when the connection is unstable. Please switch to Google Authenticator instead of phone verification. You can set it up from the gear icon at the top right of Seller Central: [Login Settings].

My listing was removed / I was asked for more documents. How do I find out why?
  1. First check the red-text alerts in Seller Central [Inventory] → product status and the "Safety and Compliance" tab. Most delistings in Europe state exactly what's missing (EU Responsible Person, EPR, test reports, labeling).
  2. Use the "Compliance Reference" tool to look up the full requirements for that category, so you can fix everything at once instead of guessing item by item.
  3. If it's still unclear, open a Seller Support case, or send the ASIN and screenshots to your account manager for help interpreting it.
What is DAC7? Does it affect me?

DAC7 is an EU platform reporting directive that requires platforms to report sellers' identity and income information to tax authorities. The practical impact on sellers: the platform will ask you to complete / confirm your tax identification information, and not doing so may affect your account.

If you receive a request in Seller Central or by email for DAC7-related information, complete it before the deadline; don't ignore it as a marketing email. For details and how to handle your specific case, please confirm with your tax agent or account manager.
